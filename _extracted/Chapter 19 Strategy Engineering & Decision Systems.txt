19.1 What Is a Trading Strategy?
Within QROS, a trading strategy is formally defined as:
A governed decision-making system that transforms validated market information into executable trading actions while respecting predefined objectives and risk constraints.
Notice what is absent from this definition.
Nowhere does it mention:
Moving averages.
RSI.
Candlestick patterns.
AI.
Those are merely components.
The strategy is the entire decision system.

19.3 Modular Design Philosophy
A strategy should never be a single block of code.
Instead, each responsibility belongs to a dedicated module.
Typical modules include:
Data Module
Acquires and validates market information.
Feature Module
Computes research-approved features.
Market State Module
Determines:
Trending.
Ranging.
High volatility.
Low liquidity.
Event-driven conditions.
Prediction Module
Produces forecasts.
Examples:
Probability of upward movement.
Expected volatility.
Regime classification.
Decision Module
Transforms predictions into proposed actions.
Examples:
Buy.
Sell.
Hold.
Reduce exposure.
Risk Module
Determines whether the proposed action is acceptable.
Questions include:
Does this violate drawdown limits?
Is portfolio exposure excessive?
Are major news events imminent?
Execution Module
Converts approved decisions into executable orders.
Monitoring Module
Observes:
Strategy health.
Performance.
Latency.
Errors.
Drift.
19.4 Decision Pipelines
A professional trading system never asks:
"Should we buy?"
Instead, it asks a sequence of questions.
Is market data valid?
↓
Is market open?
↓
Current market regime?
↓
Signal confidence?
↓
Portfolio exposure?
↓
Risk acceptable?
↓
Execution conditions favorable?
↓
Order approved?
Each question filters uncertainty.
Only a small proportion of candidate opportunities ultimately become trades.
19.5 Signal Engineering

Risk indicators.
Each signal represents evidence—not certainty.

Professional strategies rarely depend on a single signal.
Instead, evidence is combined.
Example:
Trend Model
+
Volatility Model
+
Macro Model
+

+

Instead of binary logic:

SELL

Example:

High Liquidity

78%

Confidence enables more nuanced decision-making.
19.8 Portfolio Awareness
Retail systems often treat each trade independently.
Institutional systems do not.
Every proposed trade is evaluated in the context of the existing portfolio.
Questions include:
Are positions overly correlated?
Does this increase concentration risk?
Does it improve diversification?
Is sufficient capital available?
Strategy decisions therefore extend beyond individual trades.

Potential failures include:

API outages.
Broker rejection.
Excessive latency.
Unexpected volatility.
Infrastructure failures.
Each failure should have predefined responses.

QROS Engineering Specification
Strategy Acceptance Standard (SAS-1)
Before any strategy is promoted to production, it must:
Have a documented objective.
Define inputs and outputs.
Use validated features.
Pass statistical validation.
Complete backtesting.
Complete paper trading.
Demonstrate acceptable risk-adjusted performance.
Support explainable decisions.
Integrate with the Risk Engine.
Integrate with Monitoring.
Receive Architecture Board approval.
Strategies failing any mandatory requirement remain in the research environment.
Case Study
A research team proposes a new XAUUSD strategy that combines macroeconomic event analysis, machine learning predictions, and volatility-based position sizing.
Rather than evaluating only historical returns, the Architecture Board reviews:
Data quality and feature documentation.
Predictive model validation.
Risk integration.
Portfolio interactions.
Execution performance under different liquidity conditions.
Monitoring and alerting capabilities.
Rollback procedures.
Operational documentation.
The strategy is approved only after demonstrating that it functions as a complete decision system rather than simply generating profitable signals.
Chapter Summary
Strategy engineering is the discipline that transforms independent analytical models into coordinated decision-making systems. By combining validated data, engineered features, predictive models, risk management, execution logic, and governance within a modular architecture, QROS defines a strategy as an institutional asset rather than a collection of indicators.
The emphasis shifts from maximizing trade frequency to maximizing decision quality, operational resilience, and long-term maintainability.
